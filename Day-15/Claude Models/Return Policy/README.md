# Quarterly Earnings Analysis Model Comparison

## Models Evaluated

1. quarterly-health-check (Opus)
2. quarterly_financial_analysis (Haiku)
3. quarterly_analysis (Sonnet)

---

## Final Ranking

| Rank | Model                                | Score  | Assessment                                           |
| ---- | ------------------------------------ | ------ | ---------------------------------------------------- |
| 1    | quarterly-health-check (Opus)        | 9.5/10 | Most accurate and analyst-grade                      |
| 2    | quarterly_analysis (Sonnet)          | 8.8/10 | Strong professional analysis                         |
| 3    | quarterly_financial_analysis (Haiku) | 7.5/10 | Well-structured but contains unsupported assumptions |

---

## Why Opus Wins

### Key Insight Captured

Opus was the only model that correctly quantified the impact of margin compression:

| Metric       | Last Year | This Year |
| ------------ | --------- | --------- |
| Revenue      | 100       | 112       |
| Gross Profit | 68        | 68.3      |

Despite 12% revenue growth, gross profit is essentially flat.

This is the most important analytical conclusion from the prompt.

### Strong Financial Reasoning

Opus correctly identifies:

* Negative operating leverage
* Margin deterioration
* Potential operating income contraction
* Risks associated with AI infrastructure spending
* Management narrative versus underlying financial reality

### Analyst-Level Questioning

The model asks:

* Is AI spend in COGS or OpEx?
* Is spending recurring or capitalized?
* What is the margin recovery timeline?
* Is AI monetization occurring?

These are realistic earnings-call questions.

---

## Sonnet Strengths

Sonnet provides:

* Clear structure
* Balanced discussion
* Appropriate uncertainty
* Good identification of business risks

However, it does not calculate the effect of margin compression on gross profit and therefore misses the strongest analytical conclusion.

---

## Haiku Strengths and Limitations

Haiku provides:

* Good readability
* Executive summary format
* Useful analyst checklist

However, it introduces unsupported assumptions such as:

* Industry benchmark comparisons
* Probability estimates for future scenarios
* Definitive health assessments without sufficient evidence

These reduce analytical rigor.

---

## Conclusion

For financial analysis tasks requiring analyst-style reasoning, the outputs rank:

1. Opus (Best)
2. Sonnet
3. Haiku

Opus demonstrates the strongest financial reasoning, quantitative analysis, management narrative evaluation, and analyst questioning framework. It produces the closest output to what would be expected from a professional equity research analyst.
