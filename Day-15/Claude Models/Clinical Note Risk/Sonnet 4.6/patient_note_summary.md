# Patient Note Summary & Risk Review

**Date of Review:** 2026-06-17
**Document Type:** Emergency Department Encounter Note

---

## Clinical Summary

A patient presented with a **2-day history of chest pain**. Vital signs were documented as stable. A 12-lead ECG was performed and reported as unremarkable. The patient was **discharged home** with ibuprofen (an NSAID) and advised to return if symptoms worsened. The attending clinician noted that the encounter was affected by high ED volume and time pressure.

---

## 🔴 Clinical Risks (High Priority)

### 1. Troponin Not Ordered
**Risk Level: HIGH**
Troponin was explicitly not obtained for a patient presenting with chest pain of 2-day duration. Serial troponin measurement is a cornerstone of ACS (Acute Coronary Syndrome) rule-out protocols (e.g., HEART score pathway, ESC 0h/1h/2h algorithms). A normal ECG does not exclude myocardial injury — up to 50% of NSTEMI cases may present with a non-diagnostic ECG.

> **Potential consequence:** Missed NSTEMI or unstable angina. This represents a significant patient safety risk.

### 2. NSAID Prescribed for Undifferentiated Chest Pain
**Risk Level: HIGH**
Ibuprofen is contraindicated or relatively contraindicated in several causes of chest pain, including:
- **ACS / ischaemic heart disease** – NSAIDs increase cardiovascular risk and are associated with adverse outcomes post-MI.
- **Pericarditis** – while NSAIDs are first-line for pericarditis, high-dose aspirin (not ibuprofen) is often preferred, and colchicine co-prescription is recommended.
- **Peptic ulcer / GI cause** – NSAIDs may worsen symptoms.

Prescribing ibuprofen before a definitive diagnosis was established may have been premature.

### 3. No Differential Diagnosis Documented
**Risk Level: MEDIUM**
There is no record of a differential diagnosis for the chest pain. Potentially serious conditions (ACS, aortic dissection, pulmonary embolism, pericarditis) should be considered and either excluded or documented as considered and ruled out.

### 4. No Risk Stratification Documented
**Risk Level: MEDIUM**
No formal chest pain risk stratification tool (e.g., HEART score, TIMI score, GRACE score) was documented. This is standard of care in ED chest pain presentations and supports defensible discharge decisions.

---

## 🟡 Documentation Risks (Medium Priority)

### 5. Time Pressure Documented as Rationale
**Risk Level: MEDIUM–HIGH**
The attending noted ED volume and time pressure as a contextual factor. While this reflects operational reality, documenting time pressure as a reason (implicit or explicit) for omitting a standard investigation (troponin) may constitute a medico-legal liability. It does not meet the standard of care defence in most jurisdictions.

### 6. Incomplete Vital Signs
**Risk Level: LOW–MEDIUM**
"Vitals stable" is not a substitute for documented values. Heart rate, blood pressure, respiratory rate, SpO₂, and temperature should be explicitly recorded for a chest pain presentation. "Stable" is a subjective assessment; numerical values provide an objective baseline and are required for complete documentation.

### 7. Symptom Characterisation Absent
**Risk Level: LOW–MEDIUM**
The note does not capture key chest pain descriptors required for risk stratification:
- Character (sharp, pressure, burning)
- Radiation (arm, jaw, back)
- Associated symptoms (dyspnoea, diaphoresis, nausea, syncope)
- Exertional vs. rest onset
- Prior similar episodes or cardiac history

### 8. No Follow-up Plan Specified
**Risk Level: LOW**
The discharge advice was to "follow up if symptoms worsen." This is vague. Best practice includes a defined follow-up timeframe (e.g., 24–48 hours with GP, or return to ED if symptoms recur or intensify), and ideally a safety-net plan in writing.

## Recommendations

1. **Immediate:** Consider recalling the patient for troponin measurement (0h and 3h) and reassessment, particularly if symptoms persist.
2. **Short-term:** Review NSAID prescription given unconfirmed diagnosis; consider withholding or replacing with paracetamol pending workup.
3. **Systemic:** Review ED protocols for high-volume periods to ensure minimum safety standards (troponin in chest pain) are maintained regardless of throughput pressures.
4. **Documentation:** Update the note with numeric vitals, a differential, risk score, and a specific follow-up instruction.
