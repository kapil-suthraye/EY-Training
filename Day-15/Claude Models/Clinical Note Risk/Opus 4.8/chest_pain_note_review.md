# Patient Note Review — Chest Pain Presentation

## 1. Summary

A patient presented to the ED with a **2-day history of chest pain**. Vital signs were recorded as stable and an **EKG was performed and read as unremarkable**. The patient was **discharged home with ibuprofen** and advised to return if symptoms worsened. **No troponin was ordered.** The attending documented that the workup was conducted under **time pressure due to ED volume**.

---

## 2. Clinical Risks

| # | Risk | Why it matters |
|---|------|----------------|
| 1 | **No troponin ordered** | A single EKG does not rule out acute coronary syndrome (ACS). Troponin is a core component of the chest-pain workup. Discharging undifferentiated chest pain without cardiac biomarkers is a recognized cause of missed MI. |
| 2 | **Single EKG only** | An initial unremarkable EKG can miss evolving ischemia. Serial EKGs are often indicated, especially with ongoing or recurrent symptoms over 2 days. |
| 3 | **No documented risk stratification** | No HEART score, TIMI, or equivalent; no cardiac risk factors, pain characterization (onset, quality, radiation, exertional component), or differential noted. Risk of anchoring on a "benign" read without structured assessment. |
| 4 | **Incomplete differential** | No documentation that life-threatening non-cardiac causes (PE, aortic dissection, pneumothorax, esophageal rupture) were considered or excluded. |
| 5 | **Ibuprofen for undifferentiated chest pain** | NSAIDs can mask symptoms, carry cardiovascular and GI/bleeding risk, and are relatively contraindicated in suspected cardiac disease. Prescribing before a cause is established may be inappropriate. |
| 6 | **Vague disposition criteria** | "Follow up if symptoms worsen" lacks specific, time-bound return precautions and a defined follow-up plan/timeframe. |

---

## 3. Documentation Risks

| # | Risk | Why it matters |
|---|------|----------------|
| 1 | **"Attending noted time pressure due to ED volume"** | **Highest-priority flag.** Documenting that the workup was abbreviated because of workload reads as an admission that the standard of care may not have been met. This is a significant medicolegal liability if an adverse outcome occurs. Operational constraints should be addressed through staffing/escalation channels, not the clinical record. |
| 2 | **"Vitals stable" without values** | No actual numbers (HR, BP, RR, SpO₂, temp) or trend recorded. Unverifiable and weak if the chart is later scrutinized. |
| 3 | **No pain history or exam detail** | Missing pain characterization, associated symptoms, relevant history, and physical exam findings — gaps in both clinical reasoning and the defensibility of the note. |
| 4 | **No documented decision-making rationale** | No explanation for *why* troponin was deferred or why discharge was judged safe. Absence of reasoning makes the disposition hard to defend. |
| 5 | **No shared decision-making / consent to discharge** | No record that risks of leaving were discussed with the patient. |
| 6 | **Abbreviations and formatting** | Heavy shorthand ("Pt c/o", "EKG done") and run-together text reduce clarity; ensure abbreviations are standard and unambiguous. |

---

## 4. Suggested Next Steps

- Consider whether the patient meets criteria for **recall/re-evaluation** (troponin, serial EKG, formal risk score) before this disposition stands.
- **Revise the time-pressure line** out of the clinical narrative; route any resourcing concern to the appropriate operational/quality channel.
- Add **objective vital sign values**, pain history, exam findings, a documented **differential and rationale**, and **specific return precautions** with a follow-up timeframe.
- Reconsider the **ibuprofen prescription** in light of the undifferentiated diagnosis.

---

*This review is a documentation and risk-flagging aid, not a clinical determination. Disposition and management decisions should rest with the treating clinician(s) exercising independent judgment on the full record.*
